# Hindi Flash Cards: reading guide and word bank

For children aged 10 and younger who are learning to read Hindi. Choose a level by the child's current reading skills, rather than age. All levels remain available.

## How the levels work

- **Level 1 (50 words)**: short words without vowel signs (matras). Includes familiar pronouns, actions, objects and numbers.
- **Level 2 (100 words)**: short familiar words mixing आ (ा), इ (ि) and ई (ी), plus simple independent आ words such as आम. These are mixed intentionally, so children attend to the spelling rather than assume the same vowel every time.
- **Level 3 (200 words)**: other vowel patterns (उ, ऊ, ए, ऐ, ओ, औ), independent vowels in words such as भाई and रूई, and longer familiar words such as मछली and तितली. No single-akshara words, nasal marks, nukta or joined consonants yet.
- **Level 4 (200 words)**: nasal marks (माँ, गेंद), nukta (पेड़), joined consonants (बच्चा, पुस्तक), and longer everyday words (परिवार, कबूतर). A short word can still belong here because its spelling introduces a new feature.

These are curated practice groups, not an official graded curriculum or a measured frequency ranking. Familiarity varies by family and region. The lists favor home, food, animals, actions, school and conversational words; there are no duplicates within or across levels. Word choices and exact counts are our editorial decisions, rather than a word list prescribed by the sources below.

## A guided practice routine

1. Talk about a familiar object or action before reading its word. Ask the child what it means.
2. Begin with a comfortable level. An adult can model how letters and matras combine, then let the child try aloud before using the pronunciation button.
3. As a practical session suggestion, try 5–10 words for about five minutes. Keep it relaxed and stop sooner if the child is tired; these numbers are not a research-validated dosage.
4. Revisit words that needed help. Tap the left 25% of the flash card (anywhere vertically), or use the left arrow key, to return to a recent card. The app keeps the last five words. Tap the remaining 75% or use the right arrow to move forward again. Compare similar spellings such as दिन and दिल.
5. Finish by saying a sentence with a practiced word, then reading a short sentence or story together. Word cards are one part of reading practice; connected text adds meaning and comprehension.
6. Move on when the child reads comfortably and understands the words. There is no speed test or fixed age cutoff.

The app shuffles each level without repeating a word until all words have appeared. The explored counter measures exposure in the current practice session, not mastery. Back/forward review does not add to that counter; restarting or choosing a level resets it. No claims about learning progress are inferred from clicks.

## Research used

- [Ministry of Education: NIPUN Bharat implementation guidelines (2021)](https://www.education.gov.in/sites/upload_files/mhrd/files/nipun_bharat_eng1.pdf), section 2.7 on foundational language and literacy: informed the emphasis on familiar oral vocabulary, sound–symbol relationships, decoding, comprehension and enjoyable reading experiences in the Indian context.
- [Institute of Education Sciences / What Works Clearinghouse: Foundational Skills to Support Reading for Understanding in Kindergarten Through 3rd Grade](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/21), recommendations 2–4: informed the combination of sound–letter work, word decoding/recognition and daily connected-text reading. This is primarily evidence from English literacy; the specific Hindi matra sequence here is our adaptation, not a validated Hindi intervention from that guide.

The speaker toggle mutes pronunciation and chimes, stops audio already playing, and remembers the setting. The book counter is the number of new cards shown today across all levels (including repeated words and the initial card on reload), not the number of words learned. Back/forward review does not increase that daily count. Tap **Today** to see the last 28 calendar days in a four-week activity grid, with dates on each tile. Tap a tile for its count; empty days show zero. The history uses the existing browser localStorage keys, so it remains on this browser and origin (a local file, localhost, and GitHub Pages each have separate storage).
